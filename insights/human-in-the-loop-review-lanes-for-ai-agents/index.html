Designing human-in-the-loop review lanes for AI agents

Autonomy is a dial, not a switch. How to decide what an agent may do alone, what needs a person, and how to build review that is fast.

By Snap Kodex Engineering·5 Nov 2026·6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Define explicit triggers for human review: confidence, value, novelty and irreversibility.
  • Review should be a one-screen decision with sources, not a second investigation.
  • Track review rate and override rate; both should fall as trust is earned.

In this article
  1. Four triggers worth defining
  2. Make the review a single screen
  3. Close the loop
  4. Queues need service levels
  5. Two numbers to watch

The question for most business agents is not "automate or not". It is which decisions the agent makes alone, which it proposes for approval, and which it never touches. A well-designed review lane makes the second category fast and safe.

Four triggers worth defining

  1. Low confidence. The model's own signal, or a separate classifier, says it is unsure.
  2. High value. Refunds above an amount, contracts above a size, discounts above a limit.
  3. Novelty. A first-time customer, an unseen request type, or data that fails validation.
  4. Irreversibility. Deleting data, sending external communications, moving money.

Write these as rules in code, not as instructions in a prompt. A rule can be tested and audited; a prompt can be talked around.

Make the review a single screen

A reviewer should see the request, the agent's proposed action, the sources it used and the reasoning, then choose approve, edit or reject. If reviewers have to reopen five systems to understand the case, review becomes slower than doing the work and people will rubber-stamp or avoid it.

Close the loop

Every decision is information. Approvals show where the agent is reliable; edits show how it is wrong; rejections show what to forbid. Store them with the case, feed them into your evaluation set, and use them to adjust thresholds.

Queues need service levels

A review queue that silently ages is a hidden outage. Track queue age, assign owners, define what happens when no reviewer is available and decide in advance whether the default is to wait, to escalate or to fall back to the manual process.

Two numbers to watch

  • Review rate: the share of cases sent to a person. It should fall as the system improves.
  • Override rate: the share of reviewed cases where the person changed the agent's proposal. A rising override rate is an early warning of drift.
