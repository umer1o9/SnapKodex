AI customer support triage: routing by urgency and mood

How to classify incoming requests, honour service levels, resolve the routine ones and hand over the hard ones with context.

By Snap Kodex Engineering·5 Nov 2026·7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Classify intent, urgency and sentiment separately; they drive different decisions.
  • Autopilot is earned: start with drafts that agents approve, then automate proven categories.
  • A good handover includes a summary, history, sources and a suggested reply.

In this article
  1. Three signals, three decisions
  2. Resolve what is routine
  3. Hand over with context
  4. Earn autopilot
  5. Guardrails
  6. How to know it works

Support queues mix simple questions, urgent outages and frustrated customers. Treating them identically wastes the time of your best people. AI triage sorts the queue so each request meets the right resolver at the right speed.

Three signals, three decisions

  • Intent decides who or what should handle it: billing, technical, returns, account access.
  • Urgency decides how fast: an outage for a paying plan outranks a feature question. Combine the model's reading with hard rules such as plan tier and SLA.
  • Sentiment decides how carefully: a frustrated customer may need an experienced person and a different tone, even when the issue is simple.

Resolve what is routine

For well-understood intents, such as password resets, order status and policy questions, the agent can answer directly from approved content and act in your helpdesk: update the ticket, look up the order, trigger the reset. Answers should be grounded in your knowledge base, with sources, and the agent should say when it does not know.

Hand over with context

When a human takes over, they should start ahead, not from zero. The handover includes a short summary of the issue, the customer's relevant history, the sources the agent consulted, what it already tried and a suggested reply. Time saved here often exceeds time saved by automation of the easy cases.

Earn autopilot

  1. Shadow mode. The agent classifies and drafts; nothing is sent. You measure agreement with human decisions.
  2. Assisted mode. Agents see drafts and approve or edit with one click.
  3. Autopilot by category. Categories with consistently high approval rates are automated, with sampling and review continuing.

Guardrails

  • Tone and policy rules that support leads can edit.
  • Limits on refunds, credits and promises the agent may make.
  • Immediate escalation on signals such as legal threats, safety issues or repeated contact about the same problem.

How to know it works

Track resolution rate for automated categories, handover rate, response and resolution time compared with your baseline, reopen rate and customer satisfaction. Read a sample of conversations every week; numbers hide tone problems.
